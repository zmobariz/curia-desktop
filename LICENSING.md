# Licensing

**Right now, Curia is free for everyone to use — including commercial law firms.**

Curia relies on the UK Government's **i.AI Lex** service, which is still a
**beta / experimental** service. It wouldn't be right to sell a product that
depends on someone else's beta, so for the time being Curia is offered free to
all users via the **Free Use Grant** in [`LICENSE`](LICENSE).

## How it's structured

- **Base licence:** [PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0](LICENSE) — free for
  individuals, charities, law clinics, advice and not-for-profit organisations,
  educational and public-research institutions, and government bodies, with no
  fee and nothing to sign.
- **Beta grant (current):** on top of that, the licensor currently grants
  **everyone, including for-profit organisations**, the same free rights while
  Lex remains in beta. So today there is effectively **no charge for anyone**.

## What about commercial licensing later?

The licensor keeps the option to introduce **commercial licensing for future
versions** — for example if Lex matures out of beta and there turns out to be
a real opportunity. If that happens:

- it would apply only to **future versions**, after at least 30 days' notice in
  this repository; and
- copies you already have under the current free grant stay free.

Nothing is being sold today, so there's no commercial contact to list yet. If
you're a for-profit organisation and want to discuss future commercial terms,
open an issue in the repository and the maintainer will follow up.

## Scope — important

This licensing applies to **Curia's own source code only**. It does **not**:

- relicense or restrict **i.AI Lex** (MIT, a UK Government / i.AI project) or any
  other third-party software Curia depends on — those keep their own licences; or
- grant any rights over the **underlying legal data**. legislation.gov.uk and
  GOV.UK content is under the Open Government Licence; Find Case Law is under the
  Open Justice Licence (with a separate free application required for bulk /
  "computational" use); other sources have their own terms.

See [`NOTICE.md`](NOTICE.md) for the full attribution and data-source terms.
